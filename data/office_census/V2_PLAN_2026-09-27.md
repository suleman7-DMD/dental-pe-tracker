## 1. What "resolved" means, and how success is measured

- **Resolved:** the row's latest check is `VALID`, `VALID_CORRECTED` or `NOT_CURRENT_GP` (any
  reason) and passes the v2 validator. `IDENTITY_ONLY`, `ESCALATE`, `IDENTITY_PROBLEM` and
  `NO_WEB_EVIDENCE` stay unresolved. Relabeling `listed_only` as "resolved" is rejected: it
  would hit the number without answering the question.
- **Coverage target:** at least 80% of the frozen 1,358-row set resolved, i.e. at least 1,087
  rows. Rows that become unresolved later (from the 690 unchecked) are reported separately
  with the same metric.
- **Accuracy gate (required alongside coverage):** an independent check of a random 100 v2
  resolutions finds at least 95 correct. If it doesn't, the failing rule class is rolled
  back by supersede.

## 3. The v2 design in one paragraph

**Gather structured evidence for every row by machine first, then have a Claude session
adjudicate a pre-built evidence packet.** A local batch job probes four sources per row: IEMA
(site tenants, status, administrator, suite), IDFPR (license status and specialty of every row
dentist), Google Places (by phone, and by dentist or name plus address), and a real-browser
fetch of every candidate website (on file, from Places, from Data Axle, from prior notes). It
also lists collisions with other rows. A deterministic rule engine turns the packet into a
**proposed decision naming the exact rule that fired and any missing fact**. A v2 lane in the
existing rapid tool hands out packets. The agent confirms or overrides each proposal and
spends web searches only on what the packet couldn't settle (target: 0–1 per row, down from
1.5). It records through the existing `record`, so the live Directory, supersede, removal
brake and audit log all keep working unchanged.

## 4. Policy decisions: yours to ratify (they unlock most of the gain)

**P1. The row is the place, not the business (address-anchored rows).** A directory row is a
`practice_locations` address. If a current GP office occupies the row's site (same suite, or
the only dental tenant), the row becomes that office: `VALID_CORRECTED` with its name, phone
and site, plus the signal `successor_practice` or `rebranded`. The one exception: if that
office already has its own row, this row becomes `NOT_CURRENT_GP` duplicate. Ownership
continuity is not a census question. This settles most successor/rebrand rows, many
closure-suspected rows with a successor, and many mixed rows.
*Recommended.*

**P2. Triangulated current-operation evidence for offices with no website.** `VALID` is
allowed when identity is tied (address plus phone or dentist) **and** one of:
- (a) the existing kinds: first-party site, DSO locator, or an actually-seen Maps panel;
- (b) a Places listing that is `OPERATIONAL`, tied by phone, with a review dated within 12
  months;
- (c) two independent structured signals from {Places `OPERATIONAL` tied by phone; IEMA `Open`
  facility at the site tied by phone, suite or administrator}.

An active IDFPR license only corroborates; it never counts as one of the two signals.
**Each clause switches on only after it passes Gate A:** at most 3% of known-not-open
controls hit it. *Recommended, subject to Gate A.*

**P3. Positive not-current evidence from registries.**
- `closed`: every row dentist has a non-active IDFPR status (DECEASED / NOT RENEWED /
  INACTIVE…; tie = dentist) **and** no current dental business is at the site (no IEMA
  facility; Places shows none or `CLOSED_PERMANENTLY`). If a current GP office *is* there,
  P1 applies instead. A Places `CLOSED_PERMANENTLY` listing tied by phone or name also counts.
- `home_or_registration`: a residential signal (a real-estate listing, or Places resolving
  the address to a residence, or an IEMA *mobile*-only unit), plus no dental facility at the
  site, plus the dentist tied to a practice elsewhere (IEMA administrator, Places, or web).
- `specialist_only`: every row dentist holds an IDFPR specialist license, and the Places
  type or the site confirms it.

*Recommended.*

**P4. Mixed and multi-tenant rows resolve to one tenant.** Tie priority: row suite, then row
dentist (the NPIs are what put the row at this address), then phone, then name. IEMA gives
each tenant's suite, phone and administrator, and Places gives suites. The row is recorded as
that tenant; the other tenants go into `leads` (plus signal `split_from_mixed_row`) for the
additions/building lane. If no tenant ties to the row, the dentist-elsewhere rule applies
(moved or duplicate). *Recommended.* No new decision type is needed.

**P5. Effort floor.** In v2, a row can stay unresolved only after the packet plus at least
2 targeted searches (dentist name; phone). The record must name the missing fact and the next
lever. *Recommended.*

## 6. Phases and gates

**Phase 0: probes and calibration (about ½ day after you provide the Google key).**
- Run the probes on ~500 controls (all 62 closed, 136 moved and 168 home rows, plus 150
  random VALID rows), your hand-check 100, and all 1,358 unresolved rows.
- Output: per-rule hit rate and stale-positive rate, plus projected yield per category.
- **Gate A:** Places-based rules show at most 3% stale positives on not-open controls, and the
  projected resolution rate is at least 75%. Otherwise, switch to the fallback: Maps panels via
  claude-in-chrome for the ambiguous slice, plus a larger call lane.
- **Blind test:** v2 proposals are compared against your verdicts on the hand-check 100.

**Phase 1: build the lane and pilot (about 1 day).**
- Build the validator, lane, runbook and tests. One session works 100 stratified unresolved
  rows.
- **Gate B:** at least 70% resolved; the proposal override rate is reported per rule; zero
  validator workarounds; you spot-check 20 of the pilot's resolutions on Maps and at least
  19/20 agree.

**Phase 2: sweep (1–2 days of sessions, 3–4 in parallel).**
- All unresolved rows, then the 690 unchecked rows as a v2 first pass. Daily yield by rule
  and category.
- Removal headroom: the live page is at 657/3,332 = 19.7% removed. v2 might add about 300
  removals, reaching about 29%, still under the 35% global brake.

**Phase 3: residual (only if short of 80%).**
- A prioritized call sheet (rows with a phone, a one-question script, and a recording form
  using the README's `phone_call` source family). You or a helper call about 100–200 rows.
- **Gate C:** at least 80% of the 1,358 rows resolved.

**Phase 4: accuracy audit.**
- A random 100 v2 resolutions, stratified open/removed. Checked independently: your Maps
  check for 80, phone calls for 20.
- Accept at 95/100 or better and report every error. Below that, supersede the failing rule
  class and re-run it.

## 8. Risks and how they're handled

- **Google terms.** Places content may not be cached, apart from place IDs. We store only the
  place ID, a Maps link, and **our own conclusion in our own words** (for example "Google
  business listing at this suite with matching phone, open, checked 2026-09-28"). Packets
  with Places fields live at most 30 days, are never committed (public repo), and never go
  onto the Mapbox map. This is a personal research app; the call is yours (D1).
- **Stale positives.** No clause of P2 goes live without Gate A. Every v2 check names its
  `rule_id`, so a bad rule can be superseded as a class.
- **Name-match errors in IDFPR** (common names). Closure via license needs first + last name
  plus city/ZIP agreement, and never fires when *any* matching license is active.
- **Removal brake.** The queue interleaves card types, and the brake is never bypassed.
- **The 690 unchecked rows** would each land unresolved about 43% of the time under v1 rules.
  I recommend pausing new v1 claims until the v2 lane is live (D5).
